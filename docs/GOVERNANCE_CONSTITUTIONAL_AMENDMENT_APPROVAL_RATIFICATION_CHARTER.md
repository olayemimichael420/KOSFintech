## 1. Purpose

This charter defines the dependency boundary for determining the constitutional approval and ratification requirements applicable to constitutional amendment.

It is a governance-development control artifact.

It does **not** itself:

- create constitutional authority;
- create amendment power;
- appoint an approving authority;
- appoint a ratifying authority;
- establish approval authority;
- establish ratification authority;
- grant amendment authority;

- create constitutional consequences; or
- authorize implementation.

Where this charter identifies a required constitutional determination, that determination remains **UNRESOLVED** unless established by a constitutionally authoritative source outside this document.

## 2. Dependency on Constitutional Amendment Power

Approval and ratification are downstream of constitutional amendment power.

The existence of an approval mechanism MUST NOT independently establish the power to amend the constitutional framework.

The existence of a ratification mechanism MUST NOT independently establish the power to amend the constitutional framework.

The dependency chain is:

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL SOURCE
        ↓
AMENDMENT POWER
        ↓
AMENDMENT AUTHORITY
        ↓
AMENDMENT PROPOSAL
        ↓
AMENDMENT PROCEDURE
        ↓
APPROVAL / RATIFICATION
        ↓
EFFECTIVE CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT
```

No downstream event may manufacture an unresolved upstream authority.

Where the constitutional source of amendment power remains unresolved:

**AMENDMENT APPROVAL AUTHORITY = UNRESOLVED**

**AMENDMENT RATIFICATION AUTHORITY = UNRESOLVED**

## 3. Approval and Ratification Are Distinct Constitutional Questions

Approval and ratification MUST NOT be treated as interchangeable merely because both may participate in the constitutional amendment process.

The constitutional framework MUST determine:

- whether approval is required;
- whether ratification is required;
- whether both are required;
- whether either is sufficient;
- whether they occur sequentially;
- whether they occur concurrently;
- whether one depends upon completion of the other;
- whether approval and ratification have different authorities;
- whether approval and ratification have different constitutional effects; and
- what event establishes completion of the applicable requirement.

Until these questions are constitutionally resolved:

**APPROVAL ≠ RATIFICATION**
