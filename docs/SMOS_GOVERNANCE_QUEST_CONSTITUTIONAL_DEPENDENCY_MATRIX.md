# SMOS Governance Quest — Constitutional Dependency Matrix

**Status:** PROPOSED — DEVELOPMENT CONTROL ARTIFACT
**Constitutional authority:** NOT CREATED BY THIS DOCUMENT
**Executable authorization:** NOT GRANTED
**Purpose:** Define the non-authoritative Governance Quest that discovers participant intent, stake, relationship, qualification, alignment, jurisdictional claims, requested benefits, and governance requirements without independently creating constitutional authority.

---

## 1. Controlling Principle

The SMOS Governance Quest MAY:

- ask governance questions;
- collect participant responses;
- collect supporting evidence;
- classify responses;
- assess qualification;
- assess alignment;
- identify requested relationships;
- identify requested benefits;
- identify claimed or requested jurisdiction;
- identify required authority;
- identify unresolved constitutional dependencies;
- produce a Governance Alignment Profile;
- route matters for constitutional or authorized governance determination.

The Quest MUST NOT independently:

- create constitutional authority;
- create jurisdiction;
- create a constitutional mandate;
- create a constitutional right;
- create a constitutional obligation;
- create constitutional effect;
- establish amendment power;
- establish emergency power;
- establish enforcement power;
- establish constitutional formation;
- convert a software role into constitutional authority;
- convert a questionnaire result into executable authorization.

---

## 2. Fundamental Separation

The following distinctions are mandatory:

    PARTICIPANT CHOICE
        != QUALIFICATION

    QUALIFICATION
        != ALIGNMENT

    ALIGNMENT
        != JURISDICTION

    JURISDICTION
        != AUTHORITY

    AUTHORITY
        != MANDATE

    MANDATE
        != EXECUTABLE AUTHORIZATION

A participant may request a relationship without thereby possessing that relationship.

A participant may satisfy qualification criteria without thereby possessing authority.

A participant may be aligned with a constitutional principle without thereby possessing jurisdiction or mandate.

## 4. Governance Relationship Ladder

The Quest MAY allow a participant to express a desired participation level.

It MUST NOT treat selection as authorization.

### Level 0 — Information

Participant seeks information or observation.

### Level 1 — Participation

Participant seeks recognized participation.

### Level 2 — Service

Participant seeks to provide or receive recognized services.

### Level 3 — Verification

Participant seeks verification-related participation.

### Level 4 — Administration

Participant seeks administrative capacity.

### Level 5 — Governance

Participant seeks participation in governance processes.

### Level 6 — Delegated Authority

Participant seeks authority said to derive from an established delegator.

### Level 7 — Constitutional Authority

Participant seeks authority whose source must be constitutionally established.

## 6. Participant Choice Model

The participant MAY choose:

- desired relationship;
- desired participation level;
- desired responsibilities;
- desired benefits;
- desired scope;
- desired service relationship;
- desired governance involvement;
- desired review pathway where available.

The participant's choices constitute REQUESTS or DECLARATIONS unless an applicable constitutional or organizational rule establishes a different effect.

No selection control, checkbox, API parameter, database field, or administrator action may silently convert a requested relationship into granted authority.

---

## 7. Qualification Model

Qualification SHALL remain separate from authority.

Possible qualification states:

- QUALIFIED
- PARTIALLY_QUALIFIED
- CONDITIONALLY_QUALIFIED
- NOT_QUALIFIED
- INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
- DISPUTED
- UNRESOLVED

Qualification MAY identify that a participant satisfies a condition.

Qualification MUST NOT itself establish constitutional authority unless an already-established constitutional rule expressly gives that qualification such effect.

---

## 8. Alignment Model

Alignment SHOULD be multidimensional rather than reduced initially to a single numerical score.

Example:

| Dimension | Result |
|---|---|
| Identity | ESTABLISHED |
| Capacity | ESTABLISHED |
| Intent | DECLARED |
| Stake | DECLARED |
| Competence | VERIFIED |
| Evidence | PARTIAL |
| Jurisdiction | UNRESOLVED |
| Constitutional basis | UNRESOLVED |
| Authority source | UNRESOLVED |
| Mandate | NOT ESTABLISHED |
| Execution | NOT AUTHORIZED |

A percentage score MUST NOT be interpreted as a percentage of constitutional authority.

## 9. Evidence Model

Evidence SHOULD preserve:

- source;
- provenance;
- authenticity;
- timestamp;
- subject;
- claim supported;
- corroboration;
- conflict;
- verification status;
- preservation status.

Possible evidence states:

- VERIFIED
- PARTIALLY_VERIFIED
- UNVERIFIED
- CONTRADICTED
- INSUFFICIENT
- UNRESOLVED

Software records of evidence MUST NOT automatically become constitutional proof unless the applicable constitutional framework authorizes that evidentiary effect.

## 10. Authority Dependency

Where a Quest result indicates that authority is required, SMOS SHALL identify:

1. authority sought;
2. required source;
3. competent authority;
4. jurisdiction;
5. scope;
6. delegation requirement;
7. temporal limitation;
8. evidence requirement;
9. procedural requirement;
10. constitutional effect.

If the source cannot be established:

    AUTHORITY = UNRESOLVED

    EXECUTABLE AUTHORIZATION = NOT GRANTED

## 14. Sections 41–45 Dependency Rule

The existence of Sections 41–45 in the Governance Decision Matrix does not itself establish the constitutional authority described by those sections.

Until their required determinations are established:

- amendment execution is NOT AUTHORIZED;
- emergency constitutional execution is NOT AUTHORIZED;
- constitutional enforcement is NOT AUTHORIZED;
- constitutional consequence propagation is NOT AUTHORIZED;
- constitutional formation is NOT AUTHORIZED.

The Quest MAY identify these dependencies.

The Quest MUST NOT resolve them by software inference.

## 15. Five-Gate Execution Model

Every future governance capability SHALL be evaluated through:

### Gate 1 — Eligibility

Can the participant enter the requested relationship?

### Gate 2 — Qualification

Does the participant satisfy established conditions?

### Gate 3 — Authority

Does an established source authorize the requested capacity?

### Gate 4 — Mandate

Has a competent authority conferred the specific function?

### Gate 5 — Execution

Is execution currently authorized within the applicable scope and jurisdiction?

No gate may be inferred merely because an earlier gate passed.

---

## 16. Fail-Closed Rule

Where any constitutionally necessary element is unresolved:

    NO CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT

    NO EXECUTABLE AUTHORITY

    NO RETROSPECTIVE AUTHORITY

    NO IMPLIED AUTHORITY

    NO TECHNICAL AMENDMENT

The unresolved condition SHALL remain explicitly unresolved.

## 22. Governing Development Principle

SMOS development SHALL continue while constitutional questions remain unresolved, provided that unresolved constitutional authority is not implemented as executable authority.

Development therefore proceeds on two coordinated tracks:

    COMMERCIAL / ENGINEERING
    Build -> Stabilize -> Verify -> Package -> Deploy -> Pilot -> Improve

    CONSTITUTIONAL
    Observe -> Map -> Abstract -> Classify -> Formalize -> Test -> Preserve -> Generalize

Neither track silently overrides the other.
