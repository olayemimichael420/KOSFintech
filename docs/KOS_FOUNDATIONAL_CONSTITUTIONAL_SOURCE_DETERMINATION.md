## 1. Foundational Source

The KOS constitutional framework recognizes, as its foundational constitutional premise, the Word of God as revealed in Holy Scripture, with God as the ultimate source of authority.

This foundation is expressed within the KOS constitutional model through, among other passages:

- John 1:1–3
- John 1:14
- Psalm 24:1
- Daniel 4:17
- Philippians 2:10–11

This is the proposed constitutional source principle from which subordinate KOS authority must be derived and bounded.

## 2. Authority Hierarchy

The proposed hierarchy is:

DIVINE SOURCE
→ KOS CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION
→ DERIVED GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY
→ MANDATE
→ AUTHORIZATION
→ EXECUTION

No subordinate authority may claim greater authority than its established source.

## 3. Scripture Compared With Scripture

Constitutionally significant interpretation shall not rely upon an isolated scriptural passage where the subject can reasonably be examined through other relevant passages.

The principle of Scripture compared with Scripture shall therefore be used to examine:

- textual consistency;
- contextual consistency;
- relationship between passages;
- supporting passages;
- limiting passages;
- apparent contradictions;
- interpretation and resulting constitutional implications.

An interpretation shall not become constitutionally authoritative merely because one passage has been selected to support it.

## 4. Evidentiary Discipline

A claimed constitutional proposition derived from Scripture should identify:

1. asserted proposition;
2. relevant passages;
3. contextual relationship;
4. interpretation;
5. materially relevant contrary or limiting passages;
6. reasoning;
7. constitutional implication;
8. unresolved ambiguity.

Unresolved interpretation MUST remain unresolved.

## 5. Operational-Life Examination

Where appropriate, constitutional examination may consider whether an asserted interpretation produces consequences consistent with the foundational principles being relied upon, including:

- truth;
- justice;
- accountability;
- stewardship;
- service;
- responsibility;
- consistency;
- lawful order;
- protection against abuse of authority.

Operational evidence may support examination of an interpretation but does not become an independent source of divine authority.

## 6. Human and Technical Subordination

No person, office, institution, administrator, AI system, database, algorithm, vote, document, or software mechanism becomes the source of divine authority merely by interpreting, recording, administering, or enforcing the constitutional framework.

Technical capability MUST NOT be treated as constitutional authority.

## 7. Interpretation Boundary

The framework MUST distinguish:

**WHAT SCRIPTURE ESTABLISHES**

from:

**WHAT KOS DESIGN CHOOSES TO IMPLEMENT WITHIN ITS DERIVED JURISDICTION.**

A KOS design decision MUST NOT be represented as a divine command merely because it is considered compatible with a scriptural principle.

## 8. Proof and Verification

Where applicable, constitutional examination may proceed through:

TEXTUAL EVIDENCE
→ CONTEXTUAL EXAMINATION
→ SCRIPTURE-WITH-SCRIPTURE COMPARISON
→ LOGICAL CONSISTENCY
→ OPERATIONAL CONSISTENCY
→ AUTHORITY/SCOPE DETERMINATION
→ CONSTITUTIONAL CONSEQUENCE

Evidence supports a determination; evidence itself does not manufacture authority.

## 9. Amendment Consequence

Recognition of the foundational source does NOT automatically establish amendment power.

Any amendment determination must separately establish:

1. source of amendment power;
2. conferring authority;
3. conferring constitutional act;
4. recipient/holder;
5. jurisdiction;
6. scope;
7. limitations;
8. delegability;
9. transferability;
10. self-amendment;
11. protected provisions;
12. evidence;
13. effective event and time.
