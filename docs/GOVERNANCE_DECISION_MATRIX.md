## 24. Governance Constitutional Amendment and Change Control — Constitutional Determination

### Deterministic Question

How may a constitutional governance determination itself be created, amended, superseded, suspended, corrected, or terminated without allowing ordinary application behavior to redefine the constitutional framework?

The governance constitution MUST remain distinct from executable application behavior.

No software implementation, database mutation, administrative action, deployment, configuration change, or technical permission MUST be capable of silently redefining a constitutional determination.

### Proposed Constitutional Direction

Constitutional governance determinations SHOULD be subject to an explicit and independently identifiable change-control process.

A constitutional determination MUST NOT be treated as permanently immutable merely because it has been recorded in software.

Conversely, a constitutional determination MUST NOT be treated as changeable merely because an authorized software actor can modify a corresponding record.

Constitutional change authority MUST itself be constitutionally determined.

### Constitutional Determination Identity

Each constitutional determination SHOULD have an independently identifiable identity sufficient to distinguish:

- the determination;
- its version;
- its effective status;
- its originating authority;
- its applicable scope;
- its applicable jurisdiction;
- its effective date;
- its supersession status; and
- its historical relationship to previous determinations.

Technical identifiers MUST NOT by themselves constitute constitutional identity.

### Amendment Principle

An amendment MUST be treated as a distinct constitutional event.

An amendment MUST NOT be inferred from:

- software deployment;
- database migration;
- configuration change;
- policy-file modification;
- application-role change;
- API behavior;
- administrative action;
- source-code modification;
- emergency operational action; or
- implementation convenience.

The constitutional framework MUST determine what constitutes a valid amendment.

### Amendment Authority

The constitutional determination MUST establish:

- who or what may propose an amendment;
- who or what may authorize an amendment;
- whether the originating authority is required;
- whether multiple authorities are required;
- whether affected jurisdictions must participate;
- whether affected governance subjects must participate;
- whether approval thresholds apply;
- whether verification or certification is required;
- and what evidence establishes amendment validity.

Amendment authority remains UNRESOLVED.

### Amendment Scope

The constitutional framework MUST distinguish between amendments affecting:

1. constitutional principles;
2. governance authorities;
3. Governance Mandates;
4. mandate powers;
5. jurisdictions;
6. governance objects;
7. decision procedures;
8. accountability requirements;
9. continuity rules;
10. dispute rules;
11. execution rules;
12. implementation rules; and
13. other constitutionally determined subjects.

Different amendment classes MAY require different constitutional procedures.

No amendment class is currently constitutionally authorized.

### Proposed Amendment

A proposed constitutional amendment MUST remain distinguishable from an adopted constitutional determination.

Proposal status MUST NOT itself alter the currently effective constitutional framework.

A proposed amendment MUST NOT become executable merely because:

- it exists in a database;
- it has been submitted;
- it has received technical approval;
- an application administrator has enabled it;
- a deployment contains corresponding code; or
- a configuration value has changed.

### Amendment Approval

The constitutional determination MUST establish:

- whether amendment approval is required;
- who may approve;
- whether approval must be independent of proposal authorship;
- whether multiple approvals are required;
- whether voting is required;
- whether verification is required;
- whether certification is required;
- and what constitutes final constitutional adoption.

Approval rules remain UNRESOLVED.

### Effective Date

The constitutional framework MUST determine when an adopted amendment becomes effective.

An amendment MUST NOT become effective merely because:

- it was stored;
- it was approved technically;
- it was deployed;
- a database flag changed;
- a service restarted; or
- a new application version became available.

Effective-date rules remain UNRESOLVED.

### Prospective and Retrospective Effect

The constitutional determination MUST establish whether an amendment may affect:

- future governance actions only;
- currently pending governance actions;
- previously completed governance actions;
- historical records;
- existing mandates;
- existing jurisdictions;
- existing governance objects; or
- previously certified decisions.

Retrospective constitutional effect MUST NOT be inferred.

Retrospective-effect rules remain UNRESOLVED.

### Supersession Principle

Where a constitutional determination is replaced by a later determination, the relationship between the previous and succeeding determinations MUST remain historically identifiable.

Supersession MUST NOT erase the prior determination.

The framework MUST establish:

**Previous Determination → Superseding Determination → Effective Transition**

Supersession rules remain UNRESOLVED.

### Constitutional Version Integrity

Where versioning is constitutionally required, the system SHOULD preserve a deterministic relationship between:

**Version → Content → Authority → Approval → Effective State → Supersession**

A software version MUST NOT be confused with a constitutional version.

An application release MUST NOT automatically establish constitutional amendment.

**CONFLICTING CONSTITUTIONAL DETERMINATIONS MUST FAIL CLOSED.**
