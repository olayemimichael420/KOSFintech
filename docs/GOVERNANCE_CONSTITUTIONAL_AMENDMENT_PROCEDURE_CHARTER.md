# Governance Constitutional Amendment Procedure Charter

**Status:** PROPOSED — DEVELOPMENT CONTROL ARTIFACT
**Constitutional authority:** NOT CREATED BY THIS DOCUMENT
**Amendment power:** NOT GRANTED
**Amendment authority:** NOT APPOINTED
**Amendment procedure:** NOT ESTABLISHED
**Executable authorization:** NOT GRANTED

## 1. Purpose

This charter defines the dependency boundary for determining the constitutional procedure by which an already-authorized constitutional amendment may, if constitutionally permitted, proceed toward approval, ratification, constitutional effectiveness, and implementation.

It does **not** create constitutional authority, grant amendment power, appoint an amendment authority holder, establish an amendment procedure, establish approval or ratification authority, create constitutional effect, or authorize implementation.

## 2. Dependency on Prior Constitutional Determinations

Amendment procedure is subordinate to the constitutional source of amendment power and the constitutionally established amendment authority.

The dependency chain remains:

SOURCE OF AMENDMENT POWER
↓
AMENDMENT POWER
↓
AMENDMENT AUTHORITY
↓
AMENDMENT PROCEDURE
↓
APPROVAL / RATIFICATION
↓
CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT
↓
IMPLEMENTATION

Where the upstream source, power, or authority remains unresolved, downstream amendment procedure MUST remain unresolved.

## 3. Required Procedural Determinations

Before an amendment procedure can be recognized as constitutionally valid, the constitutional framework MUST determine:

1. required stages of amendment;
2. required notices;
3. required deliberation;
4. required approvals;
5. required certifications;
6. required evidence;
7. required voting or consent where applicable;
8. required recording;
9. required publication; and
10. conditions under which an incomplete procedure fails.

Until these determinations are constitutionally established, amendment-procedure rules remain **UNRESOLVED**.

## 4. Required Stages

The constitutional framework MUST determine whether an amendment requires sequential or otherwise ordered stages and MUST establish:

- the beginning of the procedure;
- each mandatory procedural stage;
- the authority responsible for each stage;
- dependencies between stages;
- conditions for progression;
- conditions preventing progression; and
- the event constituting completion of each required stage.

No technical workflow, administrative process, or software state may independently establish a constitutionally valid procedural stage.

## 5. Notice and Publication

Where constitutional notice or publication is required, the constitutional framework MUST determine:

- who must receive notice;
- what information must be disclosed;
- the required form of notice;
- the required timing;
- the required method of publication;
- evidence that notice or publication occurred; and
- the consequences of defective or incomplete notice.

Administrative circulation, database storage, source-control publication, or software display MUST NOT independently constitute constitutional notice or publication.

## 6. Deliberation

Where constitutional deliberation is required, the constitutional framework MUST determine:

- who is entitled to deliberate;
- the jurisdiction of deliberation;
- the required subject matter;
- minimum procedural requirements;
- required duration or stages where applicable;
- required records; and
- conditions under which deliberation is constitutionally sufficient.

A software workflow, automated process, or administrative discussion MUST NOT independently establish constitutional deliberation.

## 7. Approval

Where constitutional approval is required, the constitutional framework MUST determine:

- the authority competent to approve;
- the required approval threshold;
- whether approval must be unanimous, majority-based, concurrent, sequential, or otherwise constituted;
- required evidence of approval;
- whether approval may be conditional;
- whether approval may be withdrawn;
- the effect of defective approval; and
- the relationship between approval and ratification.

An administrative acceptance, software flag, database value, API response, or deployment event MUST NOT independently constitute constitutional approval.

## 8. Certification and Evidence

Where certification or evidence is required, the constitutional framework MUST determine:

- what facts must be established;
- who may certify them;
- the required form of certification;
- the required evidentiary standard;
- the required records;
- authenticity requirements;
- integrity requirements; and
- consequences of insufficient or defective evidence.

A successful automated test MUST NOT independently prove constitutional validity.

## 9. Voting or Consent

Where voting or consent is constitutionally applicable, the constitutional framework MUST determine:

- who may vote or consent;
- eligibility requirements;
- quorum requirements where applicable;
- voting or consent threshold;
- permitted voting or consent method;
- treatment of abstention;
- treatment of invalid or disputed votes;
- evidence and recording requirements; and
- finality requirements.

No technical voting mechanism may manufacture constitutional voting authority or constitutional consent.

## 10. Recording and Publication

The constitutional framework MUST determine whether and when a constitutionally valid amendment procedure requires:

- formal recording;
- authentication;
- preservation of records;
- public or restricted publication;
- publication timing;
- identification of the authoritative record; and
- evidence of procedural completion.

A source-code commit, database migration, deployment, document publication, or API state MUST NOT independently establish constitutional completion.

## 11. Incomplete or Defective Procedure

The constitutional framework MUST determine the consequences where any mandatory procedural requirement is:

- omitted;
- incomplete;
- defective;
- unauthorized;
- improperly evidenced;
- improperly recorded;
- improperly published; or
- otherwise constitutionally insufficient.

An incomplete procedure MUST NOT be treated as constitutionally complete merely because a governance actor, administrator, software system, or operational process declares completion.

## 12. No Procedural Self-Creation

An amendment procedure MUST NOT establish its own constitutional validity merely by declaring that the procedure is valid.

No procedural mechanism may manufacture:

- amendment power;
- amendment authority;
- jurisdiction;
- approval authority;
- ratification authority;
- constitutional effectiveness; or
- implementation authorization.

PROCEDURE ≠ SOURCE OF POWER
PROCEDURE ≠ AMENDMENT AUTHORITY

unless an already-established constitutional framework expressly provides otherwise.

## 13. Software Boundary

Software MAY represent and execute a constitutionally established amendment procedure only after the underlying constitutional source, power, authority, and applicable procedure have been independently established.

Software MUST NOT:

- create amendment procedure;
- create amendment authority;
- create approval authority;
- create ratification authority;
- determine constitutional validity;
- establish constitutional effectiveness; or
- authorize implementation.

Technical capability MUST remain subordinate to constitutional authority.

## 14. Fail-Closed Rule

Where the required procedural stages, notices, deliberation, approvals, certifications, evidence, voting or consent, recording, publication, or failure conditions cannot be established:

AMENDMENT PROCEDURE = UNRESOLVED
AMENDMENT EXECUTION = BLOCKED
CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT = NOT ESTABLISHED
EXECUTABLE AUTHORIZATION = NOT GRANTED

No governance mechanism may manufacture a missing constitutional procedural requirement.
