## 17. NEXT CONTRIBUTOR INSTRUCTIONS

What must the next contributor know?

Stage 2 established the domain-language foundation.
Stage 3A demonstrated a lightweight, non-executable method for applying
domain-language reasoning during ordinary engineering.

The method is now STABILIZED rather than continuously extended.

The production platform does not depend on a DSL implementation.

The following remain deliberately deferred or unestablished:

- executable DSL parser/compiler/runtime
- universal lifecycle transition mechanism
- universal enrollment abstraction
- generic authority DSL construct
- automatic score → grade inference
- automatic grade → result inference
- automatic result → progress advancement
- Participation → Learning Evidence relationship
- participation registration/invitation/completion/role semantics

What must NOT be changed or duplicated?

Do not introduce generic abstractions solely because two domains look
structurally similar.

Do not infer semantic relationships from structural similarity.

Do not turn state vocabulary into a universal lifecycle engine.

Do not make participation imply attendance, learning, assessment,
result, progress, capacity, assignment, authority, or authorization.

Do not add permission seeding or automatic permission grants for the
new capability.

Do not remove or overwrite protected recovery artifacts.

Do not reset, clean, force-checkout, or destructively rewrite the repo.

What should be inspected before continuing?

Inspect the current repository state and identify the next genuine
capability requirement before implementation.

Draft → Inspect → Validate → Integrate → Regression-test.

Semantic Correspondence Check → Regression → Checkpoint.
