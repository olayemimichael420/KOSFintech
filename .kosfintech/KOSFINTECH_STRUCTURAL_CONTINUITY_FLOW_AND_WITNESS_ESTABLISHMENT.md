    WORD / MATTER

          ↓

The corresponding service-platform abstraction is:

    SERVICE ACT / CLAIM
            ↓

        ┌────┼────┐

## 4. Witness Is Not Verdict

The continuity model SHALL preserve the distinction:

**WITNESS ≠ WITNESS DETERMINATION ≠ WITNESS VERDICT ≠ ESTABLISHMENT**

A witness is an attributable participant in the witness process.

A witness determination records that witness's individual assessment.

A witness verdict records the collective result under the applicable witness rule.

Establishment records that the specified proposition has satisfied the applicable establishment condition.

The final result MUST NOT erase the individual provenance of the witnesses.

---

## 5. Two-of-Three Preservation Rule

Where a service has an adopted or otherwise explicitly applicable two-of-three rule, the continuity record SHOULD preserve:

- the matter or claim examined;
- the evidence package;
- Witness 1 identity and determination;
- Witness 2 identity and determination;
- Witness 3 identity and determination, where present;
- the applicable witness rule;
- the threshold determination;
- the collective witness verdict;
- the establishment result;
- the resulting verified Service Act;
- any downstream valuation;
- the TP issuance decision, where applicable;
- audit and historical provenance.

Example:

    W1 → ESTABLISH
    W2 → ESTABLISH
    W3 → DISPUTE

may satisfy a two-of-three establishment threshold while preserving W3's disagreement.

Therefore:

**2-OF-3 ≠ UNANIMITY**

and:

**ESTABLISHED ≠ NO DISAGREEMENT**

A dissent MUST NOT be silently discarded merely because the applicable threshold has been satisfied.

---

## 6. Establishment Is Scoped

Establishment SHALL remain scoped to the proposition actually examined.

**ESTABLISHMENT OF MATTER X ≠ ESTABLISHMENT OF ALL PROPOSITIONS ABOUT X**

For example, establishment that a Service Act occurred and satisfies a defined verification rule does not, by itself, establish:

- constitutional authority;
- institutional office;
- theological agreement;
- constitutional adoption;
- unlimited permission;
- ownership;
- legitimacy beyond the proposition examined.

Therefore:

**WITNESS VERDICT ≠ CONSTITUTIONAL CONFERRAL**

**VERIFICATION ≠ APPOINTMENT**

**PROOF ≠ AUTHORITY**

**ESTABLISHMENT ≠ CONSTITUTIONAL ADOPTION**

## 7. Three Establishment Questions

The semantic frontier distinguishes at least three possible propositions:

### 7.1 Identity

**WHO performed, submitted, or is attributed to the matter?**

### 7.2 Act

**WHAT happened or what Service Act was performed or claimed?**

### 7.3 Result

**WHAT result, contribution, or qualifying outcome was established?**

These may require different evidence and different verification treatment.

Therefore:

**PROOF OF IDENTITY ≠ PROOF OF ACT ≠ PROOF OF RESULT**

No one proof record SHALL be silently generalized to every related proposition.

Within the formative KOS service model:

**SERVICE PARTICIPATION SHALL NOT, BY ITSELF, ESTABLISH THE PARTICIPANT'S FAITH, THEOLOGICAL AGREEMENT, CONSTITUTIONAL ADOPTION, OR CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY.**

Conversely:

**FAITH, BY ITSELF, SHALL NOT GRANT UNBOUNDED TECHNICAL PERMISSION, INSTITUTIONAL OFFICE, OR CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY.**

Relevant distinctions include:

- BELIEF-ALIGNED;
- NON-ALIGNED;

**UNESTABLISHED ≠ NON-ALIGNED**

Alignment is not automatically an eligibility condition for every service.

A service may be:

1. open to aligned and non-aligned participants;
2. explicitly belief-specific where its terms require alignment; or
3. alignment-irrelevant.

Therefore:

**ALIGNMENT ≠ ELIGIBILITY**

**ELIGIBILITY ≠ ACCEPTANCE**

**ACCEPTANCE ≠ PERMISSION**

**PERMISSION ≠ AUTHORITY**

## 10. Authority, Capacity, Permission, Channel, Operation

The authorization stream remains distinct from the witness stream:

    AUTHORITY
        ↓
     CAPACITY
        ↓
    PERMISSION
        ↓
      CHANNEL
        ↓
     OPERATION
        ↓
    SERVICE ACT
        ↓
     EVIDENCE
        ↓
       PROOF
        ↓
   VERIFICATION
        ↓
   ESTABLISHMENT

Authority determines recognized capacity.

Capacity identifies what kind of service activity a participant may perform within context.

Permission determines whether that capacity may be exercised in the specific context.

A Channel is a recognized means through which the operation is communicated or executed.

An Operation is the action performed through the recognized channel under applicable permission.

A Service Act is a service-related act sufficiently identified, attributable, contextualized, and recorded to be treated as a unit of service contribution.

The following remain distinct:

**CHANNEL ≠ AUTHORITY**

**OPERATION ≠ SERVICE ACT**

**SERVICE ACT ≠ AUTOMATIC SUCCESS**

**SERVICE ACT ≠ CONSTITUTIONAL ACT**

Evidence supports a claim.

Establishment records satisfaction of the applicable witness or establishment condition.

None of these automatically manufactures authority.

## 17. AI Continuity Boundary

AI analysis of multiple sources is not automatically equivalent to the two-of-three witness mechanism.

**AI AGREEMENT ≠ WITNESS ESTABLISHMENT**

**AI COMPARISON ≠ CONSTITUTIONAL WITNESSING**

**AI CONSENSUS ≠ AUTHORITY**

**REPEATED AI CONCLUSIONS ≠ INDEPENDENT PROOF**

An AI provider may be a source of analysis or evidence within the development process.

If a formal service rule expressly designates an AI or AI-assisted mechanism as a witness, that designation must come from an applicable authority/rule; the AI does not acquire witness authority merely by producing an answer.
