## Purpose

This record consolidates the currently available upstream evidence relevant to the possible scope and future semantic development of CIT_MOS.

It establishes a bounded requirement space for consideration of technology-management services, service-value actions, activities, organizational structures, offices, officers, responsibilities, delegated assignments, oversight, accountability, evidence, and continuity.

This record is **not** a CIT_MOS minimum semantic contract, production service specification, legal agreement, governance authorization, constitutional determination, or implementation authorization.

- No constitutional authority.

## 11. Extensibility Principle

CIT_MOS should be extensible in scope without becoming semantically undefined.

Open-ended scope means that additional technology-management services, service-value actions, activities, organizational structures, offices, officers, responsibilities, and delegated assignments may be considered when independently justified.

Open-ended does not mean that every proposed capability automatically belongs to CIT_MOS.

Accordingly:

**Extensibility != Undefined Scope**

**Extensibility != Automatic Semantic Meaning**

**Extensibility != Automatic Authority**

**Extensibility != Automatic Permission**

**Extensibility != Automatic Implementation Authorization**

A newly proposed CIT_MOS capability should therefore be admitted through an evidence-based progression in which its requirement, domain meaning, relationships, responsibility structure, evidence obligations, validation basis, and authorization status can be separately identified.

This principle preserves future capacity without converting architectural possibility into present operational fact.

## 12. Requirement-to-Authorization Progression

The developing CIT_MOS order should preserve the following progression:

**Requirement → Semantic Contract → Validation → Engineering Correspondence → Operational Model → Tests / Verification → Evidence Closure → Authorization**

Each stage has a distinct function.

### Requirement

An identifiable need or requirement establishes why a capability should be considered.

### Semantic Contract

The requirement is translated into bounded domain meaning, including relevant entities, relationships, states, responsibilities, actions, evidence, and boundaries where applicable.

### Validation

The proposed semantics are independently examined and validated before implementation is treated as semantically warranted.

### Engineering Correspondence

Existing or proposed technical mechanisms are mapped to the validated semantic requirements rather than being allowed to define them by implementation convenience.

### Operational Model

The validated semantics are translated into the corresponding operational structures, workflows, responsibilities, and service mechanisms.

### Tests / Verification

The implementation is tested against defined behavioral, integrity, security, isolation, and other applicable requirements.

### Evidence Closure

The resulting evidence is recorded with sufficient provenance to distinguish what was proposed, implemented, tested, verified, unresolved, or rejected.

### Authorization

Implementation or operation requiring authorization proceeds only after the applicable authority has independently established that authorization.

No earlier stage automatically substitutes for a later authorization requirement.

## 14. Evidence Classification

For purposes of continuing CIT_MOS development, evidence should be distinguished at minimum into the following categories:

1. **Upstream Requirement Evidence** — evidence indicating a need or intended operational purpose.
2. **Developmental Evidence** — evidence produced through exploratory or developmental work.
3. **Semantic Evidence** — evidence supporting a defined CIT_MOS meaning or relationship.
4. **Engineering Evidence** — evidence that a technical mechanism exists or behaves as tested.
5. **Operational Evidence** — evidence arising from actual authorized operational use.
6. **Authorization Evidence** — evidence demonstrating that the applicable authority has actually granted the relevant authorization.

These categories are not interchangeable.

In particular:

**Engineering Evidence != Semantic Validation**

**Semantic Validation != Operational Authorization**

**Operational Evidence != Constitutional Authority**

**Technical Attribution != Governance Authority**

**Successful Test != Finality**

A technical artifact must therefore not be promoted into a higher authority or evidence category merely because it exists, executes successfully, is stored in a database, is assigned to a role, or has been committed to Git.

## 16. Adoption Gate

CIT_MOS shall not be treated as operational merely because existing KOSFintech engineering mechanisms can technically support a proposed capability.

Before a proposed CIT_MOS capability is adopted into an operational model, the following questions must be answerable from appropriate evidence:

1. What independent requirement establishes the capability?
2. What bounded CIT_MOS meaning does the capability have?
3. What entities, relationships, actions, states, responsibilities, and evidence obligations are required?
4. What existing or proposed engineering mechanism corresponds to those semantics?
5. What validation establishes that the correspondence is correct?
6. What operational structures are required?
7. What tests and verification establish behavioral correctness?
8. What evidence establishes the resulting state and provenance?
9. What authority, if any, is required to permit implementation or operation?
10. Where is that authorization independently recorded?

If these questions cannot be answered at the level required for the proposed capability, the capability remains developmental, unresolved, deferred, or exploratory as applicable.
