## 1. PURPOSE

This record establishes a comprehensive developmental comparison between:

1. the existing KOSFintech Development-Control / Operational Engineering Order; and
2. the independently bounded CIT_MOS Development-Service Order.

The purpose is not to replace the existing KOSFintech development-control system, invalidate its verified engineering capability, or prematurely declare CIT_MOS operational.

The purpose is to determine:

- what the existing order demonstrably provides;
- what the CIT_MOS order is expected to provide when independently established and adopted;
- which existing capabilities may correspond to future CIT_MOS requirements;
- which CIT_MOS meanings remain unresolved;
- what semantic, normative, contractual, operational, evidentiary, and authorization gates remain;
- how strategic/constitutional direction may relate to CIT_MOS progression without being confused with technical implementation authority; and
- what conditions must exist before CIT_MOS can legitimately become an operational Development Service.

No unresolved concept is promoted to production semantics by this record.

## 5. CENTRAL COMPARATIVE FINDING

The existing KOSFintech order answers substantially:

> How can consequential software development be controlled?

The CIT_MOS order must additionally answer:

> What exactly is a technology-development service, who participates in it, what service boundary contains it, what lifecycle governs it, what evidence establishes its state, and what authorization permits its operation?

Therefore:

Existing Engineering Capability != CIT_MOS Service Meaning

Existing Test Result != CIT_MOS Semantic Validation

Technical Permission != Service Authorization

Service Authorization != Constitutional Authority

## 7. MINIMUM SEMANTIC QUESTIONS

Before production implementation, CIT_MOS must independently resolve at minimum:

### 7.1 Service Definition

What exactly constitutes a CIT_MOS Development Service?

What constitutes:

- initiation;
- acceptance;
- commencement;
- progress;
- suspension;
- change;
- verification;
- completion;
- rejection;
- cancellation;
- closure?

### 7.2 Participants

What is:

- provider;
- recipient;
- participant;
- developer;
- reviewer;
- verifier;
- responsible party?

What capacity does each participant possess?

### 7.3 Organizational Structure

Are the following CIT_MOS concepts required?

- engineering unit;
- project;
- work unit;
- assignment;
- responsibility record;
- office/function;
- service team.

If required, what are their relationships?

### 7.4 Service Boundary

What is a CIT_MOS tenant?

What establishes it?

How does a technology-service environment relate to the tenant?

How does a CIT_MOS tenant relate to the wider KOSFintech platform?

### 7.5 Authorization

What may a CIT_MOS participant authorize?

What is:

- technical capability;
- participant permission;
- service-level authorization;
- operational approval;
- implementation authorization?

How are these prevented from being confused with constitutional authority?

### 7.6 Lifecycle

What are the authoritative Development Service states?

What transitions are valid?

What evidence is required for each transition?

### 7.7 Evidence

What evidence is sufficient to establish movement between service states?

Who or what validates that evidence?

How is correspondence established between the evidence and the CIT_MOS requirement?

How is that distinction maintained from merely passing an existing KOSFintech test?

## 8. NORMATIVE / CONTRACTUAL QUESTION

A legal or normative instrument may eventually be necessary for portions of the CIT_MOS operating model.

However, this record does not declare that a legal contract is already required or that a legal document can substitute for unresolved semantic definition.

Potential future instruments must be distinguished:

### Domain Semantic Contract

Defines what the CIT_MOS service concepts mean.

### Operational Charter / Operating Agreement

Defines how an established service is operated.

### Legal Development-Service Agreement

Where actual parties, rights, obligations, intellectual-property arrangements, confidentiality, liability, dispute mechanisms, governing law, or other legally enforceable matters require a legal instrument.

No such instrument is established by KOS-DEV-0010.

## 9. VISIONEER / STRATEGIC DIRECTION

The CIT_MOS adoption process must preserve the distinction between strategic/constitutional direction and technical implementation.

Where the governing KOS constitutional/project sources establish a Visioneer role in strategic direction or progression, that role may form part of the upstream progression context for establishing independent CIT_MOS requirements.

The Visioneer must not, merely by appearing in a development artifact, be converted into:

- a CIT_MOS tenant;
- a developer;
- a provider;
- an administrator;
- a technical permission holder;
- a service recipient;
- an implementation mechanism; or
- a CIT_MOS production authority.

Any authority attributed to the Visioneer must be derived from the applicable governing source and limited to its established scope.

The engineering system cannot manufacture Visioneer authority.

Conversely, strategic direction cannot by itself manufacture technical implementation.

The intended distinction is:

Strategic / Constitutional Direction
→ Independent CIT_MOS Requirement
→ Validated Semantic Meaning
→ Operational Service Definition
→ Engineering Correspondence
→ Implementation
